### EU Action Plan on Cybersecurity and Artificial Intelligence

- ENISA is mentioned as a key actor in the Action Plan and in charge of the coordination with the member states and the open source communities. From this highlevel idea, it was discussed several aspects in the potential cooperation with ENISA:  
1. Cooperation is always a good option and opportunity to make our message heard and understood, however, and based on previous experiences, it should not be a free consultancy work delivered by the open source community.  
2. It is important to support their work since definitions about open source in which the community is not involved didn’t have a good outcome in the past.  
3. It is important to align with ENISA financial support with expectations from the community, but as a community we’ll keep engaged in consultations as we’ve one until now.  
4. ENISA is engaged in the preparation of the next Code and Compliance and we’ll use this relationship to explore how this cooperation can be set up

- CRA \- AI Act interplay: The discussion highlighted a fundamental conflict between the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) and the AI Act, as the former defines open source via governance while the latter uses licensing, leading to situations where a project might qualify under one law but not the other. Jordan acknowledged that the AI Act's definition is flawed and likely requires revision to align with the G7's standards, though Matthias clarified that the "open source steward" role remains exclusive to the CRA and unaffected by AI Act requirements. Ultimately, the group recommended treating this legislative conflict as a low-priority issue for now, while acknowledging it will eventually necessitate corrective action  
- It was discussed concerns that AI-generated code is non-inspectable and poses liability risks under the Cyber Resilience Act, challenging its suitability for foundational infrastructure.  
- While some advocates noted that open source AI remains feasible with transparent training data, there is a strong emphasis on maintaining security guardrails against unpredictable code contributions.  
- The group also identified AI bots as a practical solution for filtering out low-quality or AI-generated spam from open source project pull requests

### AOB

* Matthias flagged a draft **German Bundesnachrichtendienst law** that would require the BSI (CRA market surveillance authority) to share vulnerability disclosures directly with the BND (foreign intelligence). [Draft available here](https://www.bmi.bund.de/SharedDocs/gesetzgebungsverfahren/DE/Downloads/referentenentwuerfe/OESI2/NDRefG.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1%20)  
* This creates a direct conflict: reporting a vulnerability to BSI as required by CRA could result in it being **exploited for intelligence purposes** rather than patched.   
* Matthias noted this is likely not unique to Germany, other EU member states may have similar arrangements, just not written down so explicitly.
